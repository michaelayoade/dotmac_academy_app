# How Selling Works

Selling has a reputation problem: people picture pressure, patter, and tricks.
Real selling — the kind that survives repeat business — is something else
entirely: **helping a buyer make a good decision, at a profit**. Markets
differ enormously (a boardroom deal, a shop counter, a phone enquiry, a home
visit), but the principles underneath are the same everywhere, and this course
teaches those. Where examples are needed we'll draw from several settings;
translate freely into your own.

## Selling is problem-solving

Nobody buys a product. They buy what the product *does for them*: the problem
it removes, the outcome it enables, the risk it avoids, the feeling it gives.
This is the first universal principle, and everything else in the course
follows from it:

- The seller's core activity is **understanding the buyer's situation**, not
  reciting the product's.
- A sale happens when the buyer believes, on good evidence, that your offer
  is the best available answer to *their* problem — including the option of
  doing nothing.
- The fastest way to lose trust is to push an answer before you understand
  the question. Everyone has met that seller; nobody buys from them twice.

This reframing is practical, not moral. Problem-solvers get repeat customers,
referrals, and price tolerance; pressure-sellers get one transaction and a
guarded reputation.

## Every sale moves through the same stages

Whatever your market, a sale passes through recognisable stages, even when
they compress into minutes at a counter or stretch over months in a tender:

1. **Prospecting** — finding people who might have the problem you solve.
2. **Qualifying** — establishing whether *this* person genuinely could buy:
   real need, means to pay, authority to decide, a reason to act now.
3. **Discovery** — understanding their situation and what matters in it.
4. **Proposing** — presenting your offer as an answer to what you learned.
5. **Resolving concerns** — objections, questions, negotiation.
6. **Closing** — asking for and confirming the decision.
7. **Follow-through** — delivering, checking in, earning the next sale.

Two things about the stages are universal. First, **skipping early stages
poisons late ones**: most "closing problems" are actually discovery that
never happened — the proposal answered a question nobody asked. Second, the
stages are a *diagnostic*: when a sale stalls, locate the stage where it
actually stopped, which is usually earlier than it appears.

## The pipeline: selling is a numbers process

One conversation may fail for a hundred unpredictable reasons; a hundred
conversations behave predictably. That is the pipeline principle: sales
results are governed by **how much goes in the top, and what fraction
survives each stage**.

Three consequences every seller must internalise:

- **Top-of-pipeline activity is the lever you control.** You cannot force
  any single buyer to buy, but you fully control how many new conversations
  you start this week. Results this quarter reflect prospecting from weeks
  or months ago — which is why a "sudden" drought is never sudden.
- **Conversion between stages is measurable and improvable.** If many
  proposals go out and few return, the problem is discovery or proposal
  quality; if few conversations start at all, no closing technique will
  save you. Numbers tell you *which skill* to fix.
- **A healthy pipeline needs constant feeding.** The classic trap: land a
  big deal, stop prospecting to deliver it, and hit a drought two months
  later. Prospecting is a fixed habit, not a response to emptiness.

## Effort goes where probability lives

Time is a seller's only real inventory, and the pipeline view tells you where
to spend it: on the prospects most likely to buy, at the stages where your
effort changes the outcome. That means real qualification (Chapter 2), honest
stage labels rather than optimistic ones, and the discipline to stop pursuing
deals that are dead — politely, and on purpose. Chasing everything equally
feels diligent and is actually the most common way sellers waste their year.

## Trust is the currency

Across every market, buyers decide with two questions: *does this seller
understand my situation?* and *can I believe what they tell me?* Competence
and honesty, demonstrated early and cheaply — accurate small claims, kept
micro-promises ("I'll send it by Thursday", sent by Thursday), honest "no,
that model won't do what you need" — compound into the only durable
advantage in selling. Every technique in this course works *through* trust
and fails without it.

## Summary

- Selling is helping a buyer make a good decision at a profit; understand
  the problem before offering the answer.
- Every sale moves through the same stages — prospect, qualify, discover,
  propose, resolve, close, follow through — and stalls trace back to
  skipped early stages.
- Results are a pipeline: top-of-funnel activity you control, stage
  conversions you can measure and improve, and feeding it is a habit.
- Spend effort where probability lives; qualify hard and drop dead deals
  deliberately.
- Trust — demonstrated understanding plus believable claims — is the
  currency every other technique spends.
